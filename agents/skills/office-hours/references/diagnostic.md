# Startup Diagnostic

Use these criteria to make the startup-mode verdict consistent with the evidence.

## Strong Signals

- A named person has the problem.
- The person already spends time, money, or risk on a workaround.
- The user observed the workflow directly or has credible demand evidence.
- The narrow wedge produces a complete valuable outcome.
- The user has relevant domain access or expertise.
- A falsifiable experiment can run before the full build.

## Weak Signals

- The audience is only a broad category.
- The status quo is “nothing” and no cost is visible.
- Scope expands with every question.
- Interest centers on technology rather than the user's workflow.
- Pricing, buyer, or urgency cannot be explained.
- All evidence is hypothetical.

## Verdict Guide

- **Proceed:** concrete user, costly workaround, testable wedge, and no contradicted load-bearing premise.
- **Proceed with caution:** the problem is real but one material premise remains untested.
- **Pause and gather evidence:** the user or pain is plausible, but current evidence cannot justify the proposed build.
- **Stop this approach:** the proposed solution does not address the observed problem, a cheaper status quo dominates, or several core premises are contradicted.

Do not produce a verdict by mechanically counting bullets. Explain which evidence carries the decision and what would change it.

## Assignments

Assignments should produce observable evidence:

- Watch a named user perform the current workflow without helping.
- Ask several target buyers what they currently pay and what would trigger a switch.
- Test a priced offer or concierge version before automating it.
- Compare task completion with the current workaround and the smallest proposed wedge.

Record the person, action, evidence to collect, and deadline.
